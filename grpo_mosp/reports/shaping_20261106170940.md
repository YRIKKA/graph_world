## Experimental design

The experiment tested whether discovery bonuses during group relative policy optimization (GRPO) improved multiobjective path finding on partially observed graphs. Seven agents started from the same frozen Qwen3.8-27B NF4 checkpoint with fresh LoRA adapters and training seed 101. The conditions were unshaped GRPO, five individual bonuses, and all five bonuses combined. The individual bonuses rewarded first-time edge-cost discovery, distinct completed paths, discovered hypervolume, diversity of path costs, and low overlap between paths. The edge bonus was capped at 0.01 per task, and each other bonus was capped at 0.05. [Training configuration](../full_runs/shaping_run_20261106170940/Qwen3.8-27B-unsloth-bnb-4bit_trained/shaping/launch_config.json) · [Reward definitions](../experiment_shaping/SHAPING.md)

Each condition used 32 groups of four rollouts and one optimizer epoch per group, for 128 rollouts per condition and 896 total. The agents shared eight training graphs and two validation graphs. The training curriculum increased graph size across three stages and moved from requesting one path to requesting two. Each task allowed 16 traversal attempts. Graph shuffling, balancing, and adaptive sampling were enabled. Early stopping was disabled. [Training configuration](../full_runs/shaping_run_20261106170940/Qwen3.8-27B-unsloth-bnb-4bit_trained/shaping/launch_config.json) · [Dataset manifest](../full_runs/shaping_run_20261106170940/data/manifest.json)

Evaluation paired all seven adapters and the frozen base on ten held-out graphs, one easy and one hard per domain. Each checkpoint completed one task per graph in each of two modes, for 160 tasks. Independent exploration let each checkpoint collect its own paths. Shared-transcript selection let each checkpoint choose from an identical exploration history and path archive for that graph. Both modes requested two paths with a budget of 16 attempts. The unshaped score `S` was the fraction of requested slots filled by distinct, discovered, globally Pareto-optimal paths. The distribution-shift average gives equal weight to the size, density, cost, and objective-count shifts. ## Interpretation

The pilot used one training seed, one evaluation repetition, and one graph in each domain-by-difficulty stratum. The paired bootstrap repeatedly draws the same seed and the same graph within each stratum, so its interval bounds coincide with the point estimates. Adaptive sampling could also change the order in which conditions saw the shared training graphs. The edge result is specific to this run and provides limited evidence about performance across new seeds and graphs.
